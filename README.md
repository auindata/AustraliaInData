# Australia In Data
## Public Data. Plainly Presented

In Australia public data is sparse, in different formats, difficult to access (requiring expertise and specialist tools to analyze it- often the realm of data scientists) and when you get the data requires expertise to understand what is important.

Australia's federal structure makes it worse. Federal and state agencies report the same data differently using different formats with different tools and APIs to access and understand the data.

A further challenge can be for media to use this data in their stories. Media revenue is in decline with consequent smaller news rooms and the difficulty to build and maintain staff dedicated to data driven stories. There are also more smaller independent news organisations with just a few staff. It is even harder for them to have dedicated staff to do the data analysis to support their stories.

What is missing? One place to see the facts that matter.

[Australia in Data](www.auindata.org) `www.auindata.org` is a public site for Australian data and statistics.

It brings data from Australian and international sources together in one place. It is for those who need a reliable number, want to understand how information changes over time, read a short summary and note the key points.

Sources include the ABS (Australian Bureau of Statistics), the BOM, the Reserve Bank, federal and state government departments, data.gov.au and state equivalents, and international agencies such as NASA, NOAA (National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration), IEA (International Energy Agency).

It's a work in progress.
